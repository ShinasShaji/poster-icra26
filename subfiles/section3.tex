    \block{Evaluation}
    {
        \textbf{Tasks} (static setup, 3D simulated household)

        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textbf{T1 -- Placing items:} place a mug, box, and cube into a three-shelf cupboard
            \item \textbf{T2 -- Swapping items:} swap mug and cube (requires reasoning about a temporary storage location; executed after T1)
        \end{itemize}

        \vspace{0.8em}
        \textbf{Execution Success Rate}

        \vspace{0.4em}
        \begin{tabular}{l | c | c | c | c}
            & \textbf{GPT-4.1} & \textbf{Claude 4} & \textbf{Qwen3 480B} & \textbf{DeepSeek V3.1} \\\hline
            \textbf{T1} & $100\%$ & $100\%$ & $80\%$  & $100\%$ \\
            \textbf{T2} & $44.4\%$ & $100\%$ & $66.2\%$ & $75.5\%$
        \end{tabular}

        \vspace{0.8em}
        All models reliably complete T1; T2 is more challenging (requires multi-step reasoning about a temporary swap location). \textbf{Claude 4 Sonnet achieves 100\% on both tasks}, but with high latency (30--150\,s/request).

        \vspace{0.8em}
        \textbf{Hallucinated Success}

        All models (except Claude) \emph{overestimate} their success --- they believe they have succeeded even when the world state is incorrect.
        DeepSeek V3.1 is persistently overconfident, claiming success in every trial regardless of outcome.

        \begin{tikzfigure}[Believed vs.\ actual success: GPT-4.1 (left) and Claude 4 Sonnet (right)]
            \includegraphics[width=9.5cm]{figures/confusion_matrix_GPT-4.1.png}%
            \includegraphics[width=9.5cm]{figures/confusion_matrix_Claude_4_Sonnet.png}
        \end{tikzfigure}

        \vspace{0.8em}
        \textbf{Effect of Episodic Memory}

        \begin{itemize}
            \item Memory \emph{reduces tool calls} on average for all models and tasks
            \item Success rate improvement is modest and model-dependent; hallucinated memory labels limit benefits
        \end{itemize}
    }
